Why contractors need a different kind of SEO

A contractor doesn't get walk-in traffic. Your customers find you one of three ways: the Google Map pack when they search “roofer near me” or “emergency plumber [town],” the organic results below it, or a paid lead platform like Angi or Thumbtack that sells the same lead to three of your competitors. Contractor SEO is about owning the first two so you stop paying for the third.

That changes what the work looks like. There is no single storefront address to rank — you rank for a service area, which means your Google Business Profile, service-area pages, and citations all have to agree on which towns you cover. Reviews matter more than in almost any other industry, and they have to keep coming, because the Map pack rewards recent review velocity. And demand is seasonal and event-driven: roofing spikes after a wind storm, HVAC splits between the first heat wave and the first hard freeze, and your site needs to already be ranking before that happens, not after.

A warning on “guaranteed rankings.” If a company promises your contracting business page-one in 30 days, they are selling black-hat link schemes or spam that get contractor sites penalized or deindexed. We have been doing this since 2005 with white-hat methods only — the kind of work that still ranks in three years.

What’s in a contractor SEO plan

Every plan is scoped to your trade, your service-area towns, and how competitive they are. The core work is the same:

  • Google Business Profile optimization — categories, service list, service-area towns, photos, posts, and a Q&A and review workflow. For most contractors this is the single biggest lever.
  • Service & service-area pages — one page per money service, one per priority town, each written to rank instead of thin duplicate pages that get filtered.
  • Local citations & directories — consistent name, address, phone (NAP) across Google, Bing Places, Apple Business Connect, BBB, and the trade directories that matter (Houzz, Angi profile cleanup, chamber listings).
  • Review strategy — a simple post-job process to keep reviews coming in, because the Map pack rewards recency, plus response templates.
  • On-page & schema — titles, headings, internal links, and LocalBusiness / Service / FAQ structured data on your key pages.
  • Technical & speed — mobile usability, Core Web Vitals, indexation, and fixing whatever is holding the site back.
  • Call tracking & monthly reporting — through Google Analytics and Search Console, so you can see calls and form fills, not just rankings.
