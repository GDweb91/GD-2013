04ClaudeFull service breakdown

What the service actually is

"AI Search Visibility" (industry terms: GEO / AEO — Generative/Answer Engine Optimization) answers one question for a client: when someone asks ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, or Google's AI Overview "best web designer near Everett MA" / "who fixes WordPress issues in Malden" — does this business get mentioned, and does a competitor get mentioned instead?

AI answer engines pull from a narrower, more structured signal set than classic Google ranking: schema.org/JSON-LD entity data, NAP consistency across the web, FAQ-shaped content that reads as a direct answer, review aggregators (Google Business Profile especially), and citation-worthy backlinks. This overlaps heavily with what you already do for local SEO — that's the advantage.

The deliverable (what you'd actually sell)

  1. Live AI answer test — run 5–8 realistic buyer queries against ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Google AI Overview, and Claude. Screenshot what each says. This is the single most persuasive part of the pitch — concrete, not theoretical.
  2. Entity/schema audit — is the business unambiguously machine-readable (JSON-LD completeness, NAP consistency)?
  3. Answer-shaped content check — does the site have direct, extractable Q&A content, or is everything buried in marketing prose?
  4. Competitor comparison — same queries run against 2–3 local competitors, showing the gap visually.
  5. Action plan — prioritized fixes.

Where Fable actually earns its keep

Not as the thing being tested (Claude/Fable is one of the engines you're auditing) — as the production engine behind the service:

  • Drafting the polished audit report/PDF from your raw findings in minutes instead of hours.
  • Bulk-generating the FAQ content and schema markup fixes you'd then implement for a paid follow-on (this is where the real money is — the audit is the hook, the fix is the invoice).
  • Writing the outbound pitch emails and a new service-page draft.

This is what makes it "near-zero investment" — the labor-intensive parts (writing, drafting boilerplate schema) get compressed, so a $150–300 one-time audit is still profitable per hour even at small-business pricing.

Fastest path to market

  1. Run the audit on your own site first. Real proof, real before/after story, and it directly serves your existing ranking problem — two birds.
  2. Pitch existing clients before strangers. Zero acquisition cost, warm relationship, you already have site access to implement fixes. Frame: "your competitor is starting to show up when people ask AI for a [service] in [town] — you're not, yet."
  3. Add a service page on the site (ai-search-visibility-audit-boston-ma.html style, matching the modern design system) as the landing spot for the pitch and for organic discovery of the offer itself.
  4. Price in tiers: free/cheap "AI Visibility Snapshot" (screenshots + gap report) as the hook → paid "Fix" package (schema + FAQ content implementation) as the real revenue, or fold it into existing SEO retainers as a line item.

What this actually means

Two distinct findings, not one:

  1. You win on specific-service queries, you're invisible on superlative queries. "WordPress developer Everett MA" and "local SEO services Everett MA" are queries where the business's own page is the best-matching content, so both AI and Google cite it directly. But "best web designer Everett MA" is a different query shape — AI engines (and Google) answer "best of" phrasing by leaning on third-party roundup/directory content (Thumbtack, agency listicles, review aggregators) rather than a business's own site, unless that business has strong third-party trust signals backing it up. Right now nothing backs you up for that query pattern.
  2. The zero-reviews gap is very likely the root cause of #1, and it's the cheapest fix on this whole list. Reviews are one of the heaviest-weighted trust signals both classic local-pack ranking and AI answer synthesis use to decide who gets named for "best" / comparison queries. A business with real Google/Facebook reviews starts getting pulled into those AI summaries directly instead of being displaced by directories.

Why this matters for the service you're building

This exact exercise — 5 targeted queries + a reviews check, structured into a before/after table — is the audit product. It took a few minutes, it's concrete, it's screenshot/table-able, and it tells a business owner something they didn't know about themselves in a way a generic "your SEO could be better" pitch never does. This is your case study.

It also surfaces a second, even lower-cost service line to bundle in: review generation/management (asking happy clients for Google/Facebook reviews, responding to them) is nearly free to deliver, directly fixes the gap this audit just found, and is something you could pitch as step one before the deeper schema/content work.
